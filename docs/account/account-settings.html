Account settings

Open your account settings from the dashboard: click your name in the top right, then Settings. It has four tabs.

Profile

Change your display name, see when you joined, and manage two things that matter if you care about your data:

  • Export my data. Downloads a JSON file containing your account, your workspaces, templates, runs, and information about your files. Use it to keep your own copy or to move elsewhere.
  • Cookie settings. Reopens the cookie banner so you can change your analytics consent decision, including withdrawing it.

Security

Change your password: enter your current password, then the new one twice.

If you signed up with Google, there is no password to change here. Manage sign-in through your Google account instead.

If you forgot your password

On the sign-in page, choose Forgot password? and enter your email address. If that address has an account, you get an email with a link to choose a new password. The link expires in one hour and works only once.

The page always says the same thing whether or not the address has an account, so it can't be used to find out who has one. If no email arrives, check your spam folder, then try again. Requesting a new link cancels the previous one.

Setting a new password this way signs out every device, including the one you are on. Sign back in with the new password. If your account was never verified, using the reset link verifies your email address at the same time.

You can reset a password even if you normally sign in with Google, which helps if you lose access to your Google account. It adds a password to your account and leaves Google sign-in working.

Sessions

Every browser you are signed in on appears here, with the browser it identifies itself as, the IP address it connected from, and when the session was created and expires. Your current session is marked.

Revoke any session you do not recognize, or any you are finished with. That browser is signed out immediately. You cannot revoke the session you are using; sign out instead.

API keys

API keys let your own code and scripts call Puffin Ship. See the developer API reference for what you can do with one.

To create a key, give it a name you will recognize later, then choose:

  • Scope, either all your workspaces or a single one. Scope a key to one workspace when it only needs to touch that workspace.
  • Expiry, 30 days, 90 days, 1 year, or none.

The key is shown once, immediately after it is created. Copy it then, because it cannot be shown again. If you lose it, revoke it and make a new one.

Treat a key like a password: it carries your account's access. Revoke any key you no longer use, or one that may have leaked. Revoking takes effect at once.

Keys can run pipelines and read your data, but they cannot manage your account. Changing your password, managing sessions, and creating or revoking keys all require you to be signed in as yourself in a browser.

Deleting your account

The Danger zone at the bottom of the Profile tab deletes your account. It is immediate and cannot be undone.

  • Workspaces that only you belong to are erased, along with their templates, runs, and files.
  • If you own a workspace shared with other people, you must transfer ownership first. The dialog names the workspaces blocking you. See Workspace members and roles.
  • You confirm with your password, or with your email address if you sign in with Google.

If you want a copy of your data, export it before you delete.
